Can Labor hold Victoria?

A statistical forecast of the Victorian state election, built from every published poll of this term and the results of the last nine elections. It currently says no — but it also shows you how often forecasts like it have been wrong, and what it got wrong along the way.

Ranges are 95% intervals. They are not decoration: over 195 historical election-and-horizon pairs, intervals built this way contained the true result 93% of the time when 95% was claimed. See How wrong is this usually? below.

Method

Polls alone are not a forecast

Two years from an election, polls carry little information about the result; two months out they carry most of it. So the forecast mixes the poll trend with fundamentals — what history says happens to a government of this age, in this position, with its federal counterpart in office — and the weight on each is fitted from past elections rather than chosen.

Track record

What we tried that didn't work

Four sensible-sounding improvements were built, measured honestly, and rejected. They are listed because a forecast that only reports its wins is telling you half the story.

Rejected

Fat-tailed poll noise

Discount a rogue poll in proportion to how far out it is. It does fire — 10% of polls get halved — but the forecast got no better.

Rejected

Correcting for bias

Subtract the average error of past forecasts at the same horizon. It was never distinguishable from zero, and subtracting noise made things worse at every horizon.

Not warranted

Skewed error bands

Allow the forecast to be more wrong in one direction than the other. Measured, the errors are close to symmetric and slightly thin-tailed.

Regional swings

Seats in a region move together, and they genuinely do — a third of seat-level variation is regional. It widens the seat range by only 5%, because the statewide vote dominates.

How wrong is this usually?

The most important number on this page is not the forecast, it is whether the uncertainty around it is honest. Refitting everything with each election held out, across 195 election-and-horizon pairs:

Interval claimedContained the result
50%52.3%
80%75.9%
95%93.3%

Slightly narrow at the top end, close enough that the published intervals are used as they are. This is measured on the same held-out basis the forecast itself is scored on — not on the elections the model was fitted to.

Pollsters

Who leans which way

Lean is how far a firm sits from the average pollster of its day, in Labor primary points. Variability is its poll-to-poll scatter against what sampling alone would produce. Final error is how far its last poll of a campaign sat from the actual result — no model involved.

What the forecast above does and does not use. It does separate pollster lean from the underlying trend — that is the whole point of the model — but the lean figures here come from a separate fit across several elections, so they are comparable between firms rather than being the exact values inside today's Victorian numbers. Variability is measured and then not used: the forecast weights every pollster equally. Down-weighting the noisier ones is an obvious idea that has not been tested here, and an untested idea is not in the model.

Lean is a relative position within a polling period, not a prediction of who will be right on the day. Whether it predicts final-poll error is suggestive but unproven on this data (r = 0.35, p = 0.14, 19 firms).

Caveats

What this does not know

  • The polls could be wrong together. In 2018 Victorian polls put Labor near 54 and it won 57.6. Nothing here detects a field-wide miss, and the 2018 error was larger than this forecast's entire 95% band is wide on one side.
  • Five seats are set aside. Three Green-held seats, Prahran and South-West Coast are not Labor-versus-Coalition contests, so a two-party number cannot decide them. They are assumed to stay put.
  • No local factors. Retirements, candidate quality, redistribution beyond the published margins, and campaign events are all absent.
  • One Nation's preferences are the biggest assumption here.
  • Upper house not modelled at all.
