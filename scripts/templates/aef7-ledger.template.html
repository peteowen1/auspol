AEF-7 Seat Ledger

What the columns mean

Every seat of the seven elections AE Forecasts also covered, our numbers beside theirs. Miss is the winner's actual primary minus our prediction; AEF miss is the same for them. Δ primary is our |miss| minus their |miss| — negative means we did better on the winner's primary specifically, same convention as Δ loss (our seat log loss minus theirs). Notes save for everyone. Final two is the two-candidate count: official for the federal elections and South Australia; a starred value is one we derived from published preference flows, which reproduces the official pair in 43 of 47 SA seats and lands within 0.6 points when it does. ITG final two is OUR OWN top-frequency scenario out of every final-two pairing our simulation drew: the pairing our model gave the highest chance of occurring, and (R/tcp_scenarios.R) ITG pick is defined as whichever party won the MAJORITY of that pairing's own draws — so ITG TCP% is always ≥50 by construction, it names our predicted WINNER of the pairing. ITG confidence is how often our own simulation landed on that exact pairing at all. AEF final two works differently and is NOT directly comparable pick-for-pick: it is parsed from AE Forecasts' own cached scenario data (seatTcpScenarios/seatTcpBands), and "AEF pick" names a fixed party for that scenario, not necessarily the one AEF expects to win it -- "AEF TCP%" is that named party's own share, which can sit UNDER 50, and when it does AEF's real prediction is the OTHER party in the pairing. Wentworth fed2025 is the case that surfaced this: "AEF pick" reads LNP at 43.5%, which means AEF actually predicted IND (the other finalist) to win with ~56.5% -- correctly, since IND won. "AEF confidence" is how often AEF's own simulation landed on that PAIRING occurring at all, independent of who wins it.

TCP on real pairing ("Ours err"/"AEF err" columns, and the matching headline stats) is a DIFFERENT question from ITG/AEF final two above: those score each side's own #1-guessed pairing, so a seat only counts if that guess happened to match reality (n=568 ours / 574 AEF of 660). This instead looks up, for EVERY seat with a resolved real result (632 of 660 -- 28 vic2022/nsw2023 seats have no official/derived final-two at all, a pre-existing data gap, and are excluded rather than scored as a guess), whichever party that side's own scenario table named as leader of the pairing that ACTUALLY happened -- even when that was not their #1 pick -- and compares that side's predicted share for that party against what it actually got. "TCP scenario log loss" is a further-back question: how much of the probability mass each side's own simulation put on that pairing occurring AT ALL, regardless of who it thought would lead it or win it -- floored at 1e-6 for a pairing that side's simulation never drew, same convention as every other log loss on this page. scripts/build_aef7_tcp_actual.R.

Primary RMSE (every party, weighted by actual share) scores every party's predicted primary vote in every seat, not just the eventual winner's -- weighted by how big that party's ACTUAL vote was, so a badly-misjudged fringe candidate on 1% barely moves it while a badly-misjudged front-runner does. AEF's side is not shown: their fpTrend field (their own full per-party primary prediction) sits in the cached data this page already reads but has never been parsed by any script in this repo -- a real, separate build, not a metric tweak, and queued rather than guessed at.
