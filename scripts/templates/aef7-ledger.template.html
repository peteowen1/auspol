AEF-7 Seat Ledger

What the columns mean

Every seat of the seven elections AE Forecasts also covered, our numbers beside theirs. Miss is the winner's actual primary minus our prediction; AEF miss is the same for them. Δ primary is our |miss| minus their |miss| — negative means we did better on the winner's primary specifically, same convention as Δ loss (our seat log loss minus theirs). Notes save for everyone. Final two is the two-candidate count: official for the federal elections and South Australia; a starred value is one we derived from published preference flows, which reproduces the official pair in 43 of 47 SA seats and lands within 0.6 points when it does. ITG final two / AEF final two both answer the SAME question, given the REAL final two (the "Led"/"Runner-up" columns): which of those two did that side predict to win, what did they give that party's share, and how often did that side's OWN simulation draw this exact pairing at all. "Pick" is always one of the two real finalists and "TCP%" is always ≥50 by construction, like every other 2CP number on this page -- never a fixed "party A" from either side's raw scenario-tuple ordering, which is what these columns showed before 18 Sep 2026 and could read UNDER 50 for the side's own actual favourite (AEF's Wentworth fed2025 scenario named "LNP" at 43.5%, when AEF's real prediction was IND at ~56.5%, correctly, since IND won). "Confidence" is how much of that side's OWN simulation drew this real pairing AT ALL -- independent of who it thought would win it, and independent of whether this was that side's #1-most-likely scenario overall. Mallee fed2022 is the case that motivated fixing this: our TOP scenario there was LNP v IND (0.853 confidence) but the real pairing was LNP v ALP, which our simulation only drew 9.6% of the time -- "ITG confidence" now correctly shows that 0.096, not the 0.853 belonging to a pairing that never happened.

TCP on real pairing ("Ours err"/"AEF err" columns, and the matching headline stats) is a DIFFERENT question from ITG/AEF final two above: those score each side's own #1-guessed pairing, so a seat only counts if that guess happened to match reality (n=568 ours / 574 AEF of 660). This instead looks up, for EVERY seat with a resolved real result (658 of 660 -- Port Macquarie nsw2023 and Roe wa2025 are INTRA-COALITION contests, Liberal vs National, both classified to our single "LNP" bucket with no second class to pair against; correctly excluded rather than forced into a pairing our own classification cannot represent (Roe's reference row briefly carried a wrong, implausible one -- LNP vs OTH at 97.5%% -- before this check existed). 26 other gap seats, all vic2022, were backfilled from AE Forecasts' own cached real-result data, scripts/build_aef7_tcp_backfill.R), whichever party that side's own scenario table named as leader of the pairing that ACTUALLY happened -- even when that was not their #1 pick -- and compares that side's predicted share for that party against what it actually got. "TCP scenario log loss" is a further-back question: how much of the probability mass each side's own simulation put on that pairing occurring AT ALL, regardless of who it thought would lead it or win it -- floored at 1e-6 for a pairing that side's simulation never drew, same convention as every other log loss on this page. scripts/build_aef7_tcp_actual.R. Δ TCP err and Δ confidence split this into the two separate ways a seat can go wrong: Δ TCP err is "Ours err" minus "AEF err" -- same negative-is-better convention as every other Δ column -- and answers "given the pairing that happened, whose predicted SHARE within it was closer to the truth". Δ confidence is our TCP-scenario-freq minus AEF's, for that same real pairing, and reads the OPPOSITE way round: POSITIVE means WE put more of our own simulation's probability on the pairing that actually occurred, which is the good direction here, so it is coloured on that basis rather than the page's usual sign convention. Since 18 Sep 2026's "ITG/AEF pick" fix above, Δ confidence is literally "ITG confidence" minus "AEF confidence" as shown in the visible columns -- no longer two different quantities hiding under similar names, which is what caused the Mallee fed2022 confusion this same page records below. Mirani qld2024 is the case that prompted adding these Δ columns in the first place: Δ confidence +0.34 (we were far more sure OTH_RIGHT v LNP would be the final two, and it was) but Δ TCP err about +1 (both sides misjudged the flow WITHIN that pairing by almost the same amount) -- two very different failure shapes that the single combined "Ours err" number could not tell apart.

Primary RMSE (every party, weighted by actual share) scores every party's predicted primary vote in every seat, not just the eventual winner's -- weighted by how big that party's ACTUAL vote was, so a badly-misjudged fringe candidate on 1% barely moves it while a badly-misjudged front-runner does. AEF's side comes from their seatFpBands field (indexed by the same global party index as their TCP scenarios, not the per-seat candidate index), parsed by scripts/build_aef_fptrend.R and verified against a real seat (Frankston, vic2022) before being trusted. Cell counts differ (4,620 ours / 3,578 AEF) because AEF does not classify every candidate uniquely in every seat.
