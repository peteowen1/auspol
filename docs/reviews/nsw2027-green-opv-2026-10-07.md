## 1. The post (as summarised)

Argument: under optional preferential voting (OPV) the leading candidate is helped even more than under full preferential ("only around one-in-ten seats result in a trailing candidate winning" under full preferential). Exhausted ballots lower the winning threshold and cut the flow to the trailing candidate. If the Coalition and One Nation do not recommend preferences to each other, exhaustion rises and the non-Labor vote splits, which favours a Labor re-election in March 2027.

Preference flows quoted (share of that party's preferences):

| Source | Party | To Labor | To Coalition | Exhausted |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 2025 federal (full preferential) | Greens | 87.3% | 12.7% | n/a |
| 2025 federal | One Nation | 25.8% | 74.2% | n/a |
| 2023 NSW (OPV) | Greens | 59.5% | 7.3% | 33.2% |
| 2023 NSW, 17 contested seats | One Nation | 11.7% | 26.1% | 62.2% |
| SA 2026 (full pref) | One Nation to Liberal | | about 70% | n/a |

Other figures: One Nation 2023 primaries were 58.9% "1-only", 12.8% partial, 28.2% full sequence. Coalition "1-only": 73.0% (Liberal), 75.2% (National). Three-cornered 2023 contests: Port Macquarie, 41.7% of National preferences to Liberal, 51.4% exhausted; Wagga Wagga, 44.3% of Liberal preferences to National, 47.9% exhausted.

Worked example (Labor 40, Liberal 30, One Nation 30): SA-style full preferences give Labor 49.0 / Liberal 51.0; NSW OPV flows give Labor 53.5 / Liberal 46.5 with 18.7% exhausted. Labor "needed only 40.6% of the formal vote after preferences to win."

Case studies: Burdekin 2001 (Qld; One Nation 34.9, Labor 32.9, National 25.9; Labor won on 41.7% of formal, 55.1% excluding exhausted; 60.2% of non-Labor preferences exhausted). Clarence 1999 (NSW; Labor won or lost on 50.2 v 49.8 excluding exhausted; Liberal exclusion went 5.2% Labor, 64.6% National, 30.2% exhausted).

2023 Labor results by region: Greater Sydney 40.8% (31 of 59 seats), Hunter/Illawarra 49.8% (10 of 12), Rural/Regional 22.7% (4 of 22). Coalition seats on margins of 2.3% or less: Ryde, Holsworthy, Oatley, Terrigal, Drummoyne, Winston Hills, Miranda (seven). The post gives no pendulum or seat forecast as summarised.

## 3. Comparison

Numbers are exhaustion and flow shares in per cent; "match" means within about 2 points.

| Item | Green (2023 NSW) | Ours 2023 (anchor) | Ours 2027 | Verdict |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| Greens to ALP, of all GRN ballots | 59.5 | 87.1 x (1-0.397) = 52.5 | 89.1 x (1-0.332) = 59.5 | 2027 value reproduces Green exactly |
| Greens exhausted | 33.2 | 39.7 | 33.2 | 2027 uses his number (our 2023 file differs by 6.5 points; seat set or definition unconfirmed) |
| Greens to ALP, non-exhausted only | 59.5/(59.5+7.3) = 89.1 | 87.1 | 89.1 | match |
| ONP to ALP (of all ballots) | 11.7 | 37.7 x 0.289 = 10.9 | 25.5 x 0.43 = 11.0 | match |
| ONP to ALP, non-exhausted only | 11.7/37.8 = 31.0 | 37.7 | 25.5 | differs: ours is below his by 5.5 points |
| ONP exhausted | 62.2 | 71.1 | 57.0 | ours 2027 is 5.2 points lower than his, so we assume ONP preferences stay in the count more; gap is in the direction that helps the Coalition |
| ONP to Coalition (of all ballots) | 26.1 | 71.1/28.9 derived: about 18.0 | 74.5% x 43 = about 32.0 | 2027 above his by about 6 points |

Derived rows are arithmetic on our flow file (assuming flow_alp is the ALP share of non-exhausted and the remainder goes to the Coalition); not confirmed by running `derive_tpp`.

Substantive differences:
1. Our ONP 2027 exhaust of 57.0% is the state OTH rate, not an ONP measurement; his 62.2% is measured on 17 seats. His ONP-to-Labor 31.0% of non-exhausted sits between our 2027 (25.5, federal) and 2023 (37.7).
2. He treats the Coalition-ONP preference deal as a scenario variable (recommend or not). We have a single set of ONP flows and no recommendation scenario.
3. Primary vote: he gives a three-cornered example (40/30/30); our statewide trend is ALP 30.6, LNP 24.8, ONP 19.6, i.e. nobody near 40, so his threshold arithmetic (Labor on 40.6% of formal) is not at our primaries; his point is about structure, not our central estimate. Our two-party 55.1 is on the non-exhausted basis he describes.
4. He gives no seat-count forecast (as summarised); we have none for 2027 either.
