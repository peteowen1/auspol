# Western Australia's transfers are refused

Against `docs/plans/prereg-wa-flows.md`, committed before any arm was run.
**Result: refused.** Both pre-registered signals fired, and the pre-specified
fallback was worse than the thing it was meant to rescue.

## The numbers

| arm | mean gain | SE | t | improved in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Western Australia added | −0.0316 | 0.0197 | **−1.60 SE** | 3 of 9 |
| W2 fallback (WA minus Coalition-origin exclusions) | −0.0342 | 0.0143 | **−2.39 SE** | 1 of 9 |

Per-seat log score, nine elections, clustered on the election, 8 df. Pooled
accuracy 88.08% → 87.44%. Lower log score is better, so a negative gain is a
worse forecast.

The decision rule said: negative by more than 1 SE, **refuse and investigate**.

Refusal **W2 also fired**: the pooled LNP→LNP rate came in at **36.4%** against
a threshold of 30% written down before the run (it is 16.3% now, and 55.8% in
Western Australia alone).

Where it hurts most is the jurisdiction being forecast: vic2018 −0.118 and
vic2022 −0.119, the two largest degradations of the nine.

## Why — and it is not that West Australians preference differently

The matrix estimates where an excluded candidate's pile goes. Compare that
across sources:

| from → to | Victoria alone | current pool | Western Australia |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ALP → LNP | 23.8% | 30.8% | **68.0%** |
| LNP → LNP | 38.8% | 16.3% | **55.8%** |
| ALP → GRN | 25.3% | 16.0% | **2.0%** |

Western Australia runs Liberal **against** National in rural seats. The pair
surviving to the late rounds is therefore often two Coalition candidates, and
once that is the surviving set almost every transfer resolves to LNP by
construction. Labor's preferences do not "go to the Coalition 68% of the time"
in any behavioural sense — they go there because there is frequently nobody
else left.

**So this is a difference in the shape of the contest, not in voter behaviour**,
and it is exactly the kind of thing pooling across jurisdictions assumes away.

## The fallback tells us the diagnosis was too narrow

Refusal W2 pre-specified the remedy: drop Western Australia's Coalition-origin
exclusions and keep the rest. That was aimed at the LNP row. It made things
**worse** — −2.39 SE, improving 1 election of 9 — because the distortion is not
confined to the LNP row. `ALP → LNP` at 68% against Victoria's 23.8% is the same
survivor-set artefact showing up in a row the fallback leaves untouched.

Worth stating plainly: **the pre-registered fallback was wrong about the
mechanism, and running it anyway is what showed that.** Had it been quietly
replaced with a better idea after the primary arm failed, the repo would have
gained a rescued result instead of a finding.

## What the control caught, which is the point of having one

Western Australia's earliest election predates every backtest election, so
unlike Queensland there is no set of elections that naturally admits nothing.
W1 substituted a plumbing control: switch the flows on, cap what may be
admitted at 1990, and require the run to reproduce the baseline byte-for-byte.

**It failed on the first attempt** — and was right to. The cutoff was a single
`AUSPOL_FLOW_CUTOFF` applied to every source, so the arm meant to hold Western
Australia out held **Queensland** out too and could never match a baseline that
has it. The cutoff is now per source (`AUSPOL_WA_CUTOFF`, `AUSPOL_QLD_CUTOFF`)
and the control passes byte-identically.

Note what this means: without W1 the comparison would have looked complete, and
the arms would have differed for a reason that had nothing to do with Western
Australia. The measured gain above was never affected — only the control arm
was — but nothing distinguished those two possibilities from the outside.

## What is kept

**The data.** Eight Western Australian elections are fetched, validated against
the WAEC's own declared seat counts, and stay in `external/elections/`:

- first preferences and winners for all eight, 466 seats;
- transfers for seven (2001 excluded on exhaustion, 2.27%), 1,634 exclusion
  events, of which 161 exclude a One Nation candidate.

`AUSPOL_WA_FLOWS` defaults **off** everywhere, so nothing published changes.
The first preferences are unaffected by any of this and remain usable for the
questions they answer — One Nation's district-level concentration across a
fourth jurisdiction, among others.

## For a future pre-registration, not a rescue of this one

The survivor-set diagnosis suggests dropping Western Australian **seats where
both a Liberal and a National contested**, rather than dropping LNP-origin
rows. That is a different remedy from the one W2 fixed in advance, it was
conceived **after** seeing this result, and it therefore needs its own plan
committed before it is run. Recorded here so it is not mistaken later for
something this experiment established.

The broader question it raises is worth more than the WA data: **the matrix is
keyed on party class and survivor set, and assumes classes behave alike across
jurisdictions.** Western Australia is the first case where that assumption has
been measured and found to cost something. Queensland passed the same test
(+1.55 SE); Western Australia does not.

## Process note

Both prior experiments in this repo were refused on grounds invented after the
result, which is why `CLAUDE.md` now demands a refusal section naming
directional side effects in advance. **This time the pre-registered criterion
and the pre-registered refusal both fired on their own terms, and the fallback
was run despite being expected to lose.** Nothing had to be invented afterwards.
