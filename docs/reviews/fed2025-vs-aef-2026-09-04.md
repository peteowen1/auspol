# fed2025 against AE Forecasts, done properly on the second try

2026-09-04. Requested directly by Pete: how does our fed2025 forecast compare
to AE Forecasts on seat primary RMSE, seat log loss, and TPP error. First
attempt used the wrong configuration; this corrects it and adds two real
findings along the way.

## The configuration bug in my own comparison

`scripts/backtest_candidate_fed.R`'s bare defaults are `AUSPOL_SHRINK=0` and
`AUSPOL_DEV_SLOPE_MODE=""` (plain uniform swing) -- **neither matches the
published model**, which uses `shrink=0.10` and `screened` slopes
(candidate-identity aware, salience-gated). My first comparison run used the
bare defaults. Caught investigating why Mayo (Rebekha Sharkie, a three-term
IND/Centre Alliance MP) scored a literal 0% win probability: with the
candidate-identity override (`.own_x()`, backed by `personal_prior_vote()`)
inactive, her own 31.4% prior vote never reached the projection at all. Not a
residual bug in the already-fixed IND-fold-to-OTH mechanism (`docs/reviews/
forecast-mode-2026-08-22.md`) -- simply the wrong flags on my own test
command. With the correct configuration she scores 12.7% (still an
underestimate against her actual win, but no longer structurally impossible).

**Every number below uses**: `AUSPOL_FORECAST_MODE=1 AUSPOL_FED_PAIRS=2025
AUSPOL_SHRINK=0.10 AUSPOL_DEV_SLOPE_MODE=screened`.

Also fixed: `AEF_MAP`'s "CA" mapping was guessed as a Christian-right party;
it's actually Centre Alliance (Sharkie's own registration), confirmed against
AEF's `partyName` field and remapped to IND.

## The result

| metric | ours | AE Forecasts |
|---|--:|--:|
| Seat primary vote RMSE | **4.56** | 4.64 |
| Seat primary vote MAE | **3.19** | 3.40 |
| Seat win-prob log loss | 0.3663 | **0.3025** |
| Seat win-prob Brier | 0.1128 | **0.0996** |
| Seat pick accuracy | 82.6% | **86.0%** |
| Calibration slope | 1.476 | ~1.14 (8-election historical avg) |
| National TPP error | −2.74 (as of 2026-08-22, unchanged) | −3.42 |

We are at least as good on point-estimate accuracy (primary vote, TPP);
AEF is ahead on seat win-probability sharpness. The wrongly-configured first
attempt overstated this gap (log loss 0.4488 vs 0.3025) -- the real gap is
meaningfully smaller.

## Where the misses actually live

**Not diffuse.** Mean log-loss gap by our predicted party: ALP 0.058, LNP
0.059, GRN 0.106 (n=4), IND 0.241 (n=3, small). No systemic single-party
problem once configured correctly.

**One real, concentrated pattern**: genuine 2025 independent surges in seats
with little-to-no 2022 IND history -- Calwell, Franklin, Fremantle, Calare,
Bean, Blaxland, Forrest, Monash, Farrer. Both forecasters underestimated
several of these (Calwell and Franklin: AEF also projected ~0%, a shared
miss); we are clearly worse on Fremantle, Forrest, Blaxland, McPherson,
Nicholls (9-13 point gap against AEF).

## Surge-v2 on a genuinely held-out election

fed2025 is not in surge-v2's own training pairs (fed2010/13/16/19/22,
vic2022, nsw2023, sa2026, wa2008), so testing it here is a real out-of-sample
check, not a training-set replay.

**First, a harness gap fixed before testing**: `backtest_candidate_fed.R`,
`_nsw.R`, `_sa.R` and `_vic.R` all still carried the STALE 5-pair surge-v2
training list (missing today's earlier majors-bug and person-level
prev_party fixes, and three added elections). Widened to the same 9-pair set
now used in `fit_seats_full.R` and `fit_salience_surge_v2.R`, in all four
harnesses -- the WA harness has no surge-v2 support at all yet, a separate,
unaddressed gap.

**Result**: accuracy 82.6% -> 84.6%, Brier 0.1128 -> 0.1126 (flat), log loss
0.3663 -> 0.3692 (flat, very slightly worse), calibration slope 1.476 -> 1.362
(moved toward 1.0). Primary vote RMSE overall 4.56 -> 4.45; on IND rows
specifically 8.94 -> 8.44.

**Honest reading**: a real, modest, correctly-signed improvement, not a fix.
Franklin/Fremantle/Blaxland/Forrest move from 0% to 1.1-1.4% -- nudged, not
closed, against actuals of 18-27%. Calare and Monash are completely
unmoved (not flagged as governed/permitted by the salience screen at all).
The bulk of these 2025 surges remain underestimated by both forecasters.

## What this does and does not settle

**Settles**: our fed2025 forecast is closer to AE Forecasts than previously
measured, primarily accurate but somewhat less sharp on seat probabilities;
surge-v2 generalises with a small positive effect to an election outside its
own training set.

**Does not settle**: what would actually close the sharpness gap (per
`docs/reviews/seat-calibration-2026-08-22.md`, already pre-registered and
refused as a `seat_sd`/`shrink` retune -- the knobs are not the answer, and
this session did not re-open that question) or the remaining large
primary-vote misses on genuine new-independent seats, which surge-v2 only
partially reaches.
