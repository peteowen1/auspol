# Walk-throughs: wa2017 and fed2019, day before polling day

2026-10-30. Material for the walk with Pete (NEXT-STEPS "morning pages", item b). Read-only
analysis; nothing was re-run or refitted.

**Files used and their dates.** `output/level-pred.csv` (27 Oct 14:45, the current day-before
statewide forecast the seat trees are trained on), `output/level-recent.csv` (27 Oct 15:26, the
last-28-days poll average), `output/published-predictions.csv` (28 Oct 11:08, published path),
`output/forecasts.csv` (28 Oct 11:07, seat shares before and after the trees),
`output/candidacies.csv` (26 Oct, actual votes). **Not current:** the two-party-preferred (TPP)
figures come from `docs/reviews/statewide-forecast-audit-2026-09-20.md` (20 Sep); the audit script
writes into `output/`, so I did not rerun it. `output/level-components.csv` (13 Sep) is stale (it
predates the Nationals fold: WA LNP trend shows 31.2 and fundamentals 12.0 as filler) and is not used.

How "ours" is built: poll trend plus a leave-one-out fundamentals model, mixed at weight 0.72 on
the trend for a one-day horizon (`output/projection-mix.csv`, 20 Sep, n = 42 elections). `LNP`
includes the Nationals. Statewide figures are first-preference share, vote-weighted across the
whole election; seat-mean figures are unweighted means over seats.

In plain words:

  fall about 14 points (to 49%) and Labor to rise 8 (to 37%). Labor rose 20.7 (to 49.7) and won.

- **Jandakot, Kingsley, Murray-Wellington, Pilbara.** Same shape. We took 5 to 15 points off
  the Liberal vote; it fell 16 to 21 points. Labor won all four at our probabilities of 9%, 20%,

- **Hillarys, the one that ran the other way.** The sitting Liberal member (Johnson) stood as an

  problem, not a statewide one; NEXT-STEPS says it was traced and partly shipped on 17 Oct.

All five swing seats named in NEXT-STEPS were won by Labor at our P of 0.09 to 0.39 (checked).

### 5. How much was statewide, how much seat-level

**ALP-minus-LNP primary margin, points, over 59 seats (published path). Mean error = ours minus
actual; negative means Labor too low. Closer to 0 is good.**

| piece | value | n |
|---|--:|--:|
| mean margin error (the shared, statewide-type part) | -4.01 | 59 seats |
| spread (SD) of the error around that mean (the seat-by-seat part) | 8.22 | 59 seats |
| share of squared error that is the shared part | 19% | 59 seats |
| seats with the ALP-vs-LNP primary margin on the wrong side | 8 | 59 seats |
| same, after shifting every seat by the mean error | 7 | 59 seats |

Plain-English summary. The statewide forecast missed Labor by 5.8 points (36.4 vs 42.2,
vote-weighted, 1,321,640 votes), the biggest single number here. But the published seat shares did
not inherit all of it: the seat-mean Labor share was 41.2 against 41.9 actual (n = 59), because
the seat layer sits well above the statewide level (36.4 up to 41.2; NEXT-STEPS records that this
lift is not yet traced, and I did not trace it). The Coalition over-call is the visible symptom:
seat-mean LNP 40.2 against 36.9 actual (+3.3, n = 59). Shifting every seat by the average error
fixes only 1 of the 8 wrong-side seats, so most of the seat miss was seat-level: regional and
outer-suburb seats swung 16 to 21 points against our 5 to 15. Caveat: the 19% is a variance split
on one metric, not a simulation counterfactual.

### 5. How much was national, how much seat-level

**ALP-minus-LNP primary margin, points, over 151 seats (published path). Mean error = ours minus
actual; positive means Labor too high. Closer to 0 is good.**

| piece | value | n |
|---|--:|--:|
| mean margin error (the shared, national part) | +5.10 | 151 seats |
| spread (SD) of the error around that mean | 6.45 | 151 seats |
| share of squared error that is the shared part | 38% | 151 seats |
| seats with the ALP-vs-LNP primary margin on the wrong side | 16 | 151 seats |
| same, after shifting every seat by the mean error | 8 | 151 seats |

Plain-English summary. Here more of the miss is shared. The national level was Labor +2.3 and
LNP -3.2 (vote-weighted, 14,251,250 votes) and, unlike WA, it did reach the seats: seat-mean ALP
36.5 against 33.5 actual (+3.0, n = 151), LNP 39.8 against 41.9 (-2.1). Correcting every seat for
the average error halves the wrong-side seats (16 to 8). The remainder was seat-level and
regional: Queensland (One Nation seats such as Capricornia, plus Longman, Forde, Herbert, Flynn)
and Tasmania (Bass, Braddon, Lyons) fell further than the national average. The seat shares
before the trees already had ALP at 37.8 (seat mean, n = 151) against 33.5 actual, and after the
trees 38.0, so the trees neither caused nor repaired it. Caveats: a variance split on one metric,
and wrong-side counts use primary margin, not final winners.

## What to ask Pete

1. **WA.** The Labor level miss (-5.8) was largely not inherited by the seat means, so the open
   question is why the seat layer already sits about 4.8 points above the statewide level, and
   why it is still 5 to 15 points short in the swing seats.
2. **Fed.** The audit said the lever is width rather than level. The shared national miss (38% of
   squared margin error; 16 vs 8 wrong-side seats) suggests a within-cycle correlated
   polling-error term; the seat simulation partly has this already, so the question is how much.
