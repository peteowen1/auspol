3. Mechanism.
- Starting point: AUSPOL_BYELECTION_PRIOR="blend" (scripts/published_flags.R:171). Log line 17115 of refresh.log: "BF0b by-election prior: 2 seat(s) replaced (Mulgrave, Werribee)". R/byelection_prior.R:121 sets row = 0.5*2022 + 0.5*by-election.
- 2022 (archive/2022vic-firstprefs.csv): ALP 45.4, LNP 25.3, GRN 6.8. By-election 8 Feb 2025 (byelection-results.csv): ALP 28.89, LNP 28.98, Hopper IND 14.56, GRN 7.57, VS 7.14, LC 5.5, Family First 4.34.
- Blended prior (my arithmetic, not read from a file): ALP ~37.2, LNP ~27.2. Without the by-election it would be ALP 45.4, LNP 25.3: Labor ~20 points ahead on primary, not level.
- Final: ALP 26.1 (-11 from prior; statewide ALP falls 14 vs 2022), LNP 27.6 (+0.4), ONP 22.1 (Senate-vote curve 4.8% in output/onp-senate-vic2026.csv; the statewide ONP level does the rest). No seat poll (seat-poll-blend-vic2026.csv has no Werribee row; w_ind column lists werribee=0). Seat-swing port -0.9 (seat-swing-port-vic2026.csv), small.
- The model's own view is a near three-way tie on primary (27.6 / 26.1 / 22.1). LNP wins 88% of LNP-v-ALP finals (final_two.p_leader_wins_pair 0.8847) because ONP preferences go mostly right; that, plus the LNP primary lead, is the whole 61%.
4. MODEL VIEW: what the market may be missing / strongest case against us.
- For us: Labor lost 16 primary points (45.4 to 28.9) in a by-election and the Liberal finished first on primary there (29.0 vs 28.9); a One Nation candidate was not on the 2025 ballot at all, so ONP at 22% takes votes mostly from the right and from Labor-leaning workers, and their preferences favour LNP. AEF (a rival model) also has LNP favourite, 42%.
- Against us (the worry Pete raised, and I agree): (a) the by-election was an open seat after Pallas (long-serving Treasurer) resigned, so some of that drop is lost personal vote, and a Feb 2025 protest at a state government 21 months ago; (b) the model then ALSO subtracts the full 2022-to-2026 statewide Labor fall (-14) from a baseline already half-lowered by that mood, so the same Labor weakness is partly counted twice; (c) Labor still WON the by-election (TCP margin not read; UNCONFIRMED) and its own validation of the blend was weak: error 2.78 to 2.44, only 1.4 SE (prereg-byelection-prior-2026-09-18.md, Result B), while full replacement was refused because protest swings revert (Inala, Ipswich West, Upper Hunter); half-weight still carries part of a possible reversion; (d) Labor is 26.1 vs LNP 27.6, a 1.5-point primary gap, but the model gives Labor 12% vs LNP 61%: the gap is wider in win chance than in votes because of the preference/ONP assumption, which is the seat's real sensitivity.
- Not a bug on mechanics: the 6 by-election members override (CDR1) correctly puts Werribee=ALP as sitting; Lister sitting=True in output.
- Sensitivity I could NOT run (no R allowed): a Werribee-only prior at the 2022 row. Estimate: ALP primary ~+4 to +5, LNP ~-1 to -2, which would put Labor ahead on primary and likely flip the favourite. Treat as UNCONFIRMED.
- Web: not searched (UNCONFIRMED anything about Reece Clark, Pateras, local sentiment).
5. Pattern: partly model-wide, mostly seat-specific. ONP high / LNP low vs TAB is the model-wide pattern, but here ours has LNP HIGH (61 vs 22) in the seat the model-wide pattern would predict LNP low; that is the by-election prior (plus 12% ALP means Labor is hit twice).
