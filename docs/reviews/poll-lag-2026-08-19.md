# The One Nation "lag" is not a defect. It is the model working, and it is probably too small.

Run 2026-08-19 (late) against
[../plans/prereg-poll-lag.md](../plans/prereg-poll-lag.md), committed before
anything was measured. `scripts/test_poll_lag.R`.

**Verdict: LAG IS NOT A DEFECT. Nothing changed. The item is closed.**

This retires a queue item that has been open for two days and has already
consumed two failed experiments.

## The lag is real, and small

Across 139 party-cycles with a fit and at least one poll in the final 90 days,
the trend endpoint sits **below** the recent poll mean in **88 of 139** cases,
by an average of **0.33 points**.

It is concentrated exactly where it was noticed:

| party | n | mean gap | mean level |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ALP | 33 | **+0.21** | 39.7 |
| LNP | 28 | −0.07 | 41.3 |
| GRN | 28 | −0.10 | 9.3 |
| OTH | 33 | **−1.19** | 8.4 |
| ONP | 3 | **−1.40** | 5.1 |

Major parties are tracked almost exactly. Minor parties are shaded down. That is
the pattern that prompted the complaint.

## Following the polls instead would not have helped

| | trend | recent poll mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| MAE | 1.862 | 1.755 |
| RMSE | 2.387 | **2.376** |

The poll mean is better by **0.106 MAE — 1.03 clustered standard errors**, well
inside the pre-registered 2 SE band, and the RMSE difference is 0.011. The
decision rule says: the conservatism is doing no harm, close it, change nothing.

## The one case that looks like Victoria 2026 says the lag is too small

The Victoria-like subset was defined in the plan before looking: prior under 3%,
recent polling over 10%. **The entire record contains one such case.**

| | prior | recent polls | fitted | **actual** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| WA 2017, One Nation | 0.00 | 10.3 | 7.8 | **4.9** |

The trend lagged the polls by 2.5 points — and was **still 2.9 points too
high**. The polls were 5.4 too high. In the only historically analogous case,
shrinking away from the polls helped a great deal and **was nowhere near
enough.**

Per the plan, one case is not a subset analysis and no criterion was run on it.
It is reported because it is the single most relevant observation available and
it points opposite to the change that was being contemplated.

## P3 fires: the One Nation record forbids raising One Nation

Refusal P3 was written in advance: if this analysis motivates raising One
Nation, it argues against the only direct evidence there is and must be refused.

| | prior | recent polls | fitted | actual | our error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NSW 2019 | 0.00 | 5.2 | 3.9 | 1.1 | **+2.75** |
| QLD 2004 | 8.69 | 3.9 | 3.5 | 4.9 | −1.33 |
| WA 2017 | 0.00 | 10.3 | 7.8 | 4.9 | **+2.84** |

Mean error **+1.42 — we over-state One Nation**, and in both cases with a
near-zero prior the polls over-stated it far more. Three cycles is anecdote and
the plan says so. But it is anecdote pointing one way, and the proposed fix
pointed the other.

## What this means for the live Victorian number

Our fit is **20.66** against a 23.05 mean of the 11 polls in the last 90 days,
a lag of 2.4 points. Read against WA 2017 that lag is **not too big; if
anything it is too small.**

That is not a reason to lower One Nation either — it is one case, and Victoria
2026 has something no completed cycle in this data has: **no poll named One
Nation before January 2026**, so the walk runs unobserved for three years from a
0.28% prior. The honest position is that the model's conservatism here is
supported rather than suspect, and the number stays where the polls and the walk
put it.

## Two earlier statements this corrects

- **The day-0 anchor was never the mechanism.** The anchor was blamed for
  pinning One Nation low, and `ANCHOR_K` was built and refused on that theory.
  WA 2017 had a prior of **0.00** and the model fitted **7.8** — it left the
  anchor far behind without difficulty. The lag is ordinary shrinkage toward the
  walk prior under noisy minor-party polling, not the anchor.
- **"The One Nation lag" was the wrong name for it.** It is a general
  minor-party effect (OTH −1.19 on 33 cycles, against ONP's −1.40 on 3). Framing
  it as a One Nation problem invited exactly the party-specific fix that P2
  pre-emptively refused.

## What is still not known

- **Whether Victoria's One Nation polling is real.** Every number here treats
  polls as the best available measurement. Both near-zero-prior cases in the
  record had polls over-stating One Nation by 4–5 points at the endpoint. If
  Victorian pollsters are doing the same, the model's 20.66 is still too high
  and nothing in this repo can currently tell.
- **P1 stands: this is measured at the endpoint**, where polls are densest.
  Victoria 2026 is 101 days out. A poll mean that is competitive at the endpoint
  says nothing about a longer horizon.
- **The gap does carry some signal.** `err` regressed on `gap` has slope 0.432
  and correlation 0.224 — a larger lag goes with a larger positive error, i.e.
  the model still ends up above the actual in the cases where it shrank most.
  Consistent with everything above, and another reason not to shrink less.
