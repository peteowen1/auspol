## One Nation wins the WRONG SEAT TYPE in our model (2026-08-25) — CLOSED

[reviews/onp-seat-type-asymmetry-2026-08-25.md](../reviews/onp-seat-type-asymmetry-2026-08-25.md).
Found by asking why our ONP seats differ from YouGov's — YouGov raised the
question, SA 2026 answers it, and YouGov is not treated as truth anywhere in
the review.

Our model gives One Nation **6 of 6 seats in ALP-leaning territory and 0 of 6
in LNP-leaning**. SA 2026 — the only election where the party won at this
scale — was **0 of 5 and 5 of 5**, the exact opposite.

The innocent explanation is ruled out. Among the 20 Victorian seats with the
highest federal ONP vote (our own ordering input) the split is exactly 10/10
by lean, yet mean ONP probability is **0.143 in ALP-leaning seats against
0.036 in LNP-leaning ones**. Gippsland East carries more federal ONP vote than
any seat we give the party except Morwell and scores **0.048**; Melton carries
less than all of them and scores **0.561**.

Mechanism: `shares` adds each party's statewide swing and renormalises, which
takes One Nation's gain **proportionally from everyone**. Where the Coalition
holds 58.9% it stays dominant. SA says otherwise — in the top decile of ONP
gain the Coalition fell **17.69** against Labor's **4.96**, and MacKillop's
Liberal vote collapsed 67.0 → 26.8 as One Nation took the seat.

**CORRECTION, added 2026-09-09**: this section originally ended by naming its
own next step as open — "a pre-registered test of source-weighted allocation
... unlike the two experiments that aborted for lack of power". That test was
in fact run **the same day**, in
[plans/prereg-onp-vote-sourcing.md](../plans/prereg-onp-vote-sourcing.md):
**REFUSED**, and decisively — the pre-registered confound control (within-cycle
deviation, to separate "ONP took Coalition votes" from "the Coalition lost
that election") **reversed the sign of Pete's own directional hypothesis**.
LIB → ONP was predicted to exceed ALP → ONP; the measured result was
Labor losing MORE than proportional at 4.09 clustered SE, Coalition losing
LESS. The seat-type asymmetry above still stands as an observation — SA's
wins were all Coalition-leaning, ours are all Labor-leaning — but the
proposed mechanism for it (district-level Coalition-vote-sourcing) is refuted,
not merely untested. The reconciling hypothesis the refusal review offers (a
uniform statewide Coalition COLLAPSE in SA, orthogonal to district-level ONP
concentration) is itself unidentifiable from a 7-cluster corpus and remains
open, but it is a different, harder question than the one this section
originally pointed at.
